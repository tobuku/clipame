Rates by Project Type

Video editing rates depend heavily on what you're editing. Here's what each type typically costs.

Short-Form Clips (TikTok, Reels, Shorts)

$25 - $150 per clip

Under 60 seconds. Includes cuts, captions, transitions, and basic sound editing. Most common type of freelance editing work in 2026. High volume, fast turnaround.

YouTube Videos (10-20 min)

$150 - $500+ per video

Full edits with cuts, B-roll, color correction, sound mixing, and motion graphics. Longer turnaround. Higher-paying but more competitive.

Podcast Clips

$30 - $100 per clip

Clipping highlights from long podcast episodes. Typically 30-90 seconds with captions, speaker labels, and waveform graphics. Popular with podcast networks.

Gaming Clips

$20 - $75 per clip

Highlights from streams and gameplay. Usually 15-60 seconds with effects, zooms, and meme edits. Lower per-clip rate but high volume.

Brand/Ad Content

$200 - $1,000+ per video

Promotional videos for brands and products. Higher production value, revision rounds, and brand guidelines. Premium rates with longer timelines.

Monthly Retainer (Short-Form)

$500 - $3,000/month

Ongoing editing for a creator's social media. Typically 15-30 clips per month across TikTok, Reels, and Shorts. Steady income, less client hunting.

What Affects Your Rate

Experience and Portfolio

Editors with a strong portfolio of client work command higher rates. Testimonials and case studies (views generated, engagement rates) matter more than years of experience alone. One viral clip in your portfolio is worth more than 50 mediocre ones.

Niche Specialization

Specialists earn more than generalists. Finance and business content pays the most, followed by tech and SaaS. Gaming and entertainment clips pay less per unit but offer higher volume. Pick a niche and own it.

Turnaround Speed

Fast turnaround commands premium rates. Same-day delivery for breaking news or trending topics can justify 50-100% rush charges. Creators value editors who can deliver while content is still relevant.

Technical Skills

Basic cuts and captions are the floor. Editors who add motion graphics, color grading, sound design, and custom animations charge significantly more. Learning tools like AI editing software also increases throughput.

Client Type

Individual creators have smaller budgets than brands and agencies. Enterprise clients pay the most but expect more polish and revision rounds. Start with creator clients, then move to brands as your portfolio grows.

Geography

US and UK clients pay the highest rates. European and Australian clients fall in the middle. Editors in lower cost-of-living countries can undercut on price, but quality and communication are what retain clients long-term.

How to Set Your Rates

Setting rates is one of the hardest parts of freelancing. Here's a practical approach:

1. Calculate your floor

Figure out the minimum you need to earn per hour to make freelancing worthwhile. Include taxes (set aside 25-30%), software costs, and the time you spend on admin, client communication, and revisions -- not just editing time.

2. Research the market

Check what editors at your skill level charge. Browse our directory, Fiverr, Upwork, and X/Twitter. The table at the top of this page is a good starting point. Position yourself in the middle of your experience tier, not the bottom.

3. Start with per-project pricing

Per-project rates are easier for clients to budget and let you earn more as you get faster. Track how long each project takes so you know your effective hourly rate. If it drops below your floor, raise your prices.

4. Raise rates every 3-6 months

As your skills and portfolio improve, your rates should too. Give existing clients 30 days notice before a rate increase. Some will leave. That's fine -- the new clients you attract at higher rates will be better fits.

5. Offer monthly retainers

Retainers provide steady income and reduce the time you spend finding clients. Offer a 10-15% discount on per-clip rates for clients who commit to a monthly volume. This is how most full-time freelance editors structure their business.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much do freelance video editors charge per video?

Freelance video editors charge $25 to $500+ per video depending on length, complexity, and experience. Short-form clips (under 60 seconds) for TikTok or Reels typically cost $25 to $75 each. Long-form YouTube videos (10-20 minutes) range from $150 to $500+. Rates increase with motion graphics, color grading, sound design, and fast turnaround.

How much do freelance video editors charge per hour?

Hourly rates for freelance video editors range from $25 to $150+ in 2026. Beginners charge $25 to $40/hour, mid-level editors $40 to $75/hour, and experienced specialists $75 to $150+/hour. Most short-form video editors prefer per-video pricing since they can edit clips quickly, making per-video rates more profitable.

How much should I charge as a beginner video editor?

Beginner video editors typically charge $25 to $50 per short-form clip or $25 to $40 per hour. Start at the lower end to build your portfolio and collect testimonials. Once you have 5-10 completed client projects, raise your rates by 25-50%. Within 3-6 months of consistent work, most editors move into the mid-tier range.

Do video editors charge per video or per hour?

Both pricing models are common. Per-video pricing is standard for short-form content (TikTok, Reels, Shorts) because clips are quick to produce and clients want predictable costs. Hourly pricing works better for long-form projects, ongoing retainers, or complex work where scope may change. Monthly retainers ($500-$3,000/month) are popular for creators who need consistent editing.

How much do TikTok video editors make per month?

TikTok video editors (clippers) earn $500 to $5,000+ per month depending on their client base and volume. Clippers on platforms like Vyro and Clipping.net earn based on views (CPM model), typically $500 to $3,000/month. Freelance editors working directly with creators charge per clip and can earn $2,000 to $5,000+ with 3-5 regular clients.
